How it works

Three steps. No setup.

01

Give it a link

Ask Owly on Pond to test your site. No script, no SDK, no account on our side.

02

It uses your site

As a new visitor, an impatient phone user and someone with only a keyboard — in a real browser.

03

You get the answer

One sentence, the failing step, the screen, the failing request — and an issue you can paste into GitHub.

What it catches

The things people hit in the first five minutes.

The main journey

Whether a newcomer can finish sign up, checkout or booking — and which step stopped them.

Forms that fail

Submissions that error on the server, submissions that do nothing at all, fields that accept a nonsense email.

Errors in the console

Uncaught exceptions with the line that threw, failed requests and slow ones.

Broken on phones

Sideways scrolling, text cut off, buttons covered by something else at 390px.

Keyboard and access

Focus you cannot see, dialogs you cannot leave, and a WCAG 2.1 A/AA scan.

The third time

A save that works twice and fails on the third go. Owly repeats things people repeat.

Safety

It touches nothing you would not want touched.

A tester loose in your product is only useful if you can predict what it will do.

Never presses the dangerous thing

Pay, delete, send, invite, publish, unsubscribe — it stops and says so instead.

Never leaves your origin

Not by link, not by redirect, not by anything the page tries on its own.

Obvious test data only

Every field gets something like owly.qa+…@example.com. Nothing real, ever.

Your site, proven yours

Until you publish a token we give you, Owly only looks — it will not type or press.
